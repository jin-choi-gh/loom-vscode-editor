[ prose: before ]

Blake's poem is often read as the ruin of a rose by a worm. Harold Bloom's
reading is less kind to the rose. This commentary follows it, in words that
are not his.

[ verse: rose ]

O Rose thou art sick.
The invisible worm,
That flies in the night
In the howling storm:

Has found out thy bed
Of crimson joy:
And his dark secret love
Does thy life destroy.

[ prose: after ]

The bed is already "of crimson joy" when the worm finds it out. The joy is
not the worm's doing: the rose had it before, alone, in a bower shut to
everyone else.

So the rose is less innocent than it seems. What the worm finds out is a
pleasure that was being kept, a secret older than the worm's own.
